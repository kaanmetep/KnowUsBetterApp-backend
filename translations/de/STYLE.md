# German (de) — for couples and friends in Germany, Austria and Switzerland

Standard German (Germany) with ß. Natural spoken German of 20-30 year olds, like a German
indie app wrote it. Not stiff, not "Denglisch" beyond what Germans really say (Date, Netflix,
Story, Selfie, Single are fine; "Partner Quiz" style English compounds are not).

## Address
- The player: "du" / "dein" / "dir" / "dich" (lowercase). Both players together: "ihr" / "euch" / "euer".
- Never "Sie".

## The partner
- Default "dein Schatz" (warm, gender-neutral in use even though grammatically masculine).
  Never "dein Partner" / "deine Partnerin" / "Partner*in" / "Freund/Freundin" for the partner.
- Where "dein Schatz" sounds odd (hypothetical or future partner, neutral statements), use
  "die Person, mit der du zusammen bist", "dein Gegenüber", or rephrase with "ihr beide" / "zu zweit".
- Never refer back to the partner or to {{playerName}} with "er"/"sie"/"ihm"/"ihr"/"sein"/"ihr".
  Repeat the noun, repeat {{playerName}}, or restructure.
- In just_friends the other player is a friend: avoid "Freund"/"Freundin" for them too; use
  "ihr beide", "dein Gegenüber", or rephrase.

## Gender-neutral patterns
- Predicative adjectives don't inflect in German, so "Bist du eifersüchtig?" is fine for everyone.
- Avoid person nouns with male/female forms (Koch/Köchin, Frühaufsteher/in, Langschläfer/in,
  Fan/-in is fine). Use verbs ("Kochst du gern?", "Stehst du früh auf?", "Schläfst du gern lange?")
  or genuinely neutral nouns ("Morgenmensch", "Nachteule", "Person").
- Other people (friends, exes, strangers, "someone") may use the ordinary generic:
  "deine Freunde", "jemand", "dein Ex" — but prefer neutral plurals ("deine Freunde") and "jemand".

## {{playerName}}
- Never put a genitive -s on the name ("{{playerName}}s Lieblingsessen" breaks for Max, Lukas).
  Use "von {{playerName}}" or make the name the subject:
  "Was ist das Lieblingsessen von {{playerName}}?", "Was isst {{playerName}} am liebsten?"
- Names work as subject, object and after prepositions without change ("mit {{playerName}}").

## Fixed frames
- Who is more likely: "Wer würde eher …?" with the verb at the end
  ("Wer würde eher einen Jahrestag vergessen?"). This is the standard German name of the game.
- Rank It: "Ordne … von … bis …" / "Sortiere … von am … bis am …"
  ("Ordne diese Dinge danach, worauf du einen Monat lang am schwersten verzichten könntest.").
- Two truths topics: "Deine Kindheit", "Gerichte, die es bei dir zu Hause immer gab".

## Grammar and typography checklist
- Nouns capitalised; verb-second / verb-final word order; correct case after prepositions;
  separable verbs split correctly; "dass" vs "das"; "seid" vs "seit"; comma before subordinate clauses.
- Umlauts and ß always (ä ö ü ß), never ae/oe/ue/ss substitutes.
- Quotation marks „so“. Numbers: 1.000 (thousands dot), 3,5 (decimal comma), 20 °C, 5 km, 30 €.
- Dates "14. Februar". Times "20 Uhr".
- Trivia: German exonyms (Peking, Mailand, Moskau, Neuseeland, Die Simpsons only if that's the German title).
- Intimate categories: "Intimität", "Nähe", "Zärtlichkeit", "Lust", "im Bett" are fine;
  no vulgar slang, no clinical terms.
- Keep it short; German runs long, so cut filler words before cutting meaning.

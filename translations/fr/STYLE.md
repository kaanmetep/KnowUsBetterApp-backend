# French (fr) — for couples and friends in France, Belgium, Switzerland and Québec

Natural spoken French of 20-30 year olds, like a French app wrote it. Casual, warm, a bit playful.
Standard France French that also reads naturally in Belgium, Switzerland and Québec: avoid
France-only slang (meuf, mec, kiffer is OK sparingly) and Québec-only words. Not literal, not stiff.

## Address
- The player: "tu" (te, toi, ton/ta/tes). Both players together: "vous deux" / "vous" (votre, vos).
- Never "vous" for a single player.

## The partner
- Default "ta moitié" or "ton amour" (both used for anyone, the grammatical gender of the noun does
  not reveal the person's gender). Alternatives: "la personne que tu aimes", "l'autre".
- Never "ton/ta partenaire" (the determiner reveals gender), "ton copain/ta copine",
  "ton chéri/ta chérie", "ton mec/ta meuf", "petit(e) ami(e)".
- Never refer back to the partner or {{playerName}} with il/elle, le/la (person object), lui is
  OK only as indirect object (lui = to him/her, neutral), never "il/elle". Repeat or restructure.
- In just_friends the other player is a friend: "ton ami/ton amie" is gendered — use "vous deux",
  "la personne avec qui tu joues", or rephrase.

## Gender-neutral patterns (the main difficulty in French)
Adjectives, past participles with "être", and many person nouns agree with gender. Avoid them for
the player, {{playerName}}, the partner and "vous deux".
- Adjectives -> verbs or nouns: "Es-tu jaloux ?" -> "Tu ressens de la jalousie ?" / "La jalousie, ça
  te parle ?"; "Es-tu prêt ?" -> "C'est parti ?"; "fatigué" -> "Tu manques d'énergie ?".
- Epicene adjectives (-e for both: drôle, triste, calme, timide, fiable, sympa, honnête, romantique,
  jeune, sensible, aimable, facile, stable) are SAFE. -eux/-euse, -if/-ive, -é/-ée, -ant/-ante are not.
- Passé composé with "être" agrees (tu es allé/allée) -> use "avoir" verbs ("Tu as déjà voyagé seul"
  NO: seul agrees -> "Tu as déjà voyagé sans personne ?") or "Ça t'est déjà arrivé de + infinitif ?".
  Pronominal verbs agree too (tu t'es senti/sentie): avoid in the passé composé.
- "Serais-tu prêt(e) à" -> "Tu accepterais de… ?" / "Ça te dirait de… ?".
- Person nouns -> verbs: "Es-tu un bon cuisinier ?" -> "Tu te débrouilles en cuisine ?";
  "lève-tôt" is epicene (OK); "Es-tu un couche-tard" -> "Tu te couches tard ?".
- For "vous deux": "Vous êtes prêts ?" is gendered -> "On y va ?" / "À vous de jouer !".
- Other people (friends, exes, strangers): ordinary generic is fine: "tes amis", "quelqu'un", "ton ex"
  ("ex" with "ton" is accepted as generic; prefer "ton ex" over "ton/ta ex").

## {{playerName}}
- Names never inflect. Avoid any adjective/participle agreeing with {{playerName}}:
  "{{playerName}} est plus heureux quand…" -> "{{playerName}} se sent mieux quand…" / "Qu'est-ce qui
  rend {{playerName}} heureux" NO -> "Qu'est-ce qui fait plaisir à {{playerName}} ?".
- "de {{playerName}}" is fine ("Le plat préféré de {{playerName}}"). Elision: never "d'{{playerName}}"
  — names may start with a consonant; write "de {{playerName}}" always.
  Same for "que {{playerName}}" (never "qu'{{playerName}}") and "le/la" before the name (none).

## Fixed frames
- Who is more likely: "Qui est le plus susceptible de + infinitif ?" is the known name of this game
  in French ("Qui est le plus susceptible d'oublier un anniversaire ?"). "Qui" with generic
  masculine "le plus susceptible" is accepted as neutral here (like "chi dei due" in Italian).
  Shorter alternative allowed when the infinitive is long: "Qui de vous deux pourrait le plus… ?" —
  keep ONE frame per category, prefer the first.
- Rank It: "Classe du plus … au moins …" / "Classe de … à …".
- Two truths topics: "Ton enfance", "Les plats qu'on cuisinait toujours chez toi".

## Grammar and typography checklist
- French punctuation: a space (non-breaking, U+00A0, or a normal space) BEFORE ? ! : ; and inside
  « guillemets ». Use « … » for quotes. Apostrophe ' (straight is fine).
- Accents on capitals are fine (À, É). Correct agreement of everything not about the players,
  subjonctif after "que" in wishes/opinions, conditionnel correct (tu ferais, tu irais, tu voudrais).
- "Qu'est-ce que", "Est-ce que" are fine in casual questions; also "Tu préfères… ?" intonation questions.
- Numbers: 1 000 (space), 3,5, 20 °C, 30 €. Dates "14 février".
- Trivia: French exonyms (Pékin, Londres, Moscou, Nouvelle-Zélande, Munich, Pays-Bas).
  Give "accepted" without accents too (Pekin) since people type without them.
- Intimate categories: sensual and playful, never vulgar or clinical.

# Japanese (ja) — for couples and friends in Japan

Natural casual Japanese of people in their 20s-30s, like a Japanese couple app wrote it
(LINE / Instagram tone). Friendly タメ口 (plain form), not keigo, not textbook, not anime.
This is the language that needs the most care: never translate literally, rewrite the question
the way a Japanese person would actually ask it. Short.

## Address
- The player: no pronoun. Drop "you" (Japanese omits it). Never あなた, 君, お前.
  "好きな食べ物は？" not "あなたの好きな食べ物は何ですか？".
- Both players together: "ふたり" / "2人" ("ふたりの初デートはどこ？"). Pick "ふたり" (hiragana) consistently.
- Plain form: 〜したことある？ / 〜どっち？ / 〜はどう？ / 〜するなら？ Not です/ます.

## The partner
- Default "恋人" (neutral). Alternatives: "相手", "パートナー" (also neutral).
- Never 彼氏 / 彼女 / 彼 / 旦那 / 奥さん / 嫁 for the partner or {{playerName}}.
- Never refer back to {{playerName}} or the partner with 彼/彼女. Repeat the name or drop the subject.
- In just_friends the other player is a friend: "相手" / "ふたり".

## {{playerName}}
- Never add さん/くん/ちゃん. Particles go directly after it: "{{playerName}}の好きな映画は？",
  "{{playerName}}が一番緊張するのは？".
- Questions about {{playerName}} keep {{playerName}} as topic/subject; no pronoun after.

## Fixed frames
- Who is more likely: "〜しそうなのはどっち？" ("記念日を忘れそうなのはどっち？").
  For states: "〜なのはどっち？". Keep this frame for every item.
- Rank It: "〜順に並べて" ("好きな順に並べて", "大事だと思う順に並べて"). Keep the direction.
- Two truths topics: short noun phrases: "子どもの頃のこと", "実家でよく出てきた料理".
- Yes/no questions: "〜したことある？", "〜できる？".

## Typography and words
- Full-width Japanese punctuation: 、。？！「」. Question marks: full-width "？". No spaces between words.
- Half-width digits (3, 20, 100). Units: 20℃ or 20度, 30ユーロ / 3,000円 when converting money makes sense.
- Katakana loanwords only where Japanese people really use them (デート, スマホ, ドラマ, カフェ, SNS, ネトフリ is
  slang — write Netflix). Kanji at a normal adult reading level; avoid rare kanji (use hiragana).
- Answer options: short noun phrases or plain forms, parallel, no 。 at the end.
- Brands: as known in Japan (マクドナルド, スタバ is casual but OK in options, ディズニー).
- Trivia: Japanese standard names (北京, ロンドン, モスクワ, ニュージーランド). "accepted": main form first,
  then other common writings (katakana/hiragana/kanji variants), then the English name.
  Typed answers are compared after normalization, so include the realistic ways people type it.
- Intimate categories: sensual and playful (ドキドキ, ムード), never vulgar or clinical; no explicit words.
- Cultural fit: if a reference doesn't exist in Japan (Thanksgiving, prom, Turkish food), adapt to a
  Japanese equivalent with the same feel and flag it with a note.

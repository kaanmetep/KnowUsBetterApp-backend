# Dutch (nl) — for couples and friends in the Netherlands and Flanders

Natural spoken Dutch of 20-30 year olds, like a Dutch app wrote it. Casual, warm, direct, a bit
playful. Standard Dutch that reads naturally in both the Netherlands and Flanders (avoid
Netherlands-only slang like "gezellig" overuse is fine; avoid "gij"/Flemish-only words). Not literal.

## Address
- The player: "je/jij" (jou, jouw, je). Both players together: "jullie" (jullie, jullie twee).
- Never "u".

## The partner
- Default "je partner" (neutral in Dutch). Alternatives: "je lief" (warm, used for anyone;
  common in Flanders and understood in NL), "je geliefde".
- Never "je vriend/je vriendin" (gendered), "je man/vrouw", "je vriendje/vriendinnetje".
- Never refer back to the partner or {{playerName}} with hij/zij/hem/haar/zijn. Repeat the name,
  use "die" (demonstrative, neutral: "Wat doet {{playerName}} als die boos is?") sparingly, or rephrase.
- In just_friends the other player is a friend: "je vriend/vriendin" is gendered — use "jullie",
  "de persoon met wie je speelt", or rephrase.

## Gender-neutral patterns
Dutch adjectives don't mark gender, so the main risks are pronouns and person nouns.
- Person nouns with male/female forms (kok, lerares, vriendin, zanger/zangeres): rephrase with a verb:
  "Ben je een goede kok?" -> "Kun je goed koken?".
- Other people (friends, exes, strangers): ordinary generic is fine: "je vrienden", "iemand", "je ex".

## {{playerName}}
- Names never inflect: no genitive -s ("{{playerName}}s"). Use "van {{playerName}}":
  "Het lievelingseten van {{playerName}}".

## Fixed frames
- Who is more likely: "Wie van jullie zou eerder + infinitief?" ("Wie van jullie zou eerder een
  jubileum vergeten?"). Keep this frame for every item.
- Rank It: "Zet in volgorde van meest … naar minst …" / "Rangschik van … naar …".
- Two truths topics: "Je jeugd", "Het eten dat er thuis altijd op tafel stond".

## Grammar and typography checklist
- Word order (V2 in main clauses, verb-final in subordinate clauses), "dat/wat", de/het articles,
  d/t/dt verb endings (je wordt, word je), spelling of compounds as one word (zondagochtend), ë/ï
  where needed (ideeën, financiële).
- Quotation marks “…” or '…'; be consistent, prefer “…”.
- Numbers: 1.000, 3,5, 20 °C, € 30. Dates "14 februari".
- Trivia: Dutch exonyms (Peking, Londen, Moskou, Nieuw-Zeeland, München, Parijs).
- Intimate categories: sensual and playful, never vulgar or clinical.
